Kira Avatar — Design Brief

Defines what the avatar needs to express and why, ahead of final art/animation production. Direction confirmed: human-like, stylized rather than photoreal — see the concept sketch on the avatar concept page. This supersedes the earlier abstract "Lantern" exploration (still visible on the dining mockup for reference).

1. What the avatar needs to express

Five states, design and animate as one continuous character, not five disconnected poses:

StateGuest-facing meaningDesign note
IdleAmbient, waitingSubtle looping motion (breathing/blink-style), not static — must never look frozen/broken
Wake"I'm listening to you now"Clear, fast perk-up — the moment a guest decides the tablet heard them
ListeningActively hearing speechMust read clearly even with a live subtitle overlapping it
ThinkingProcessing (covers the response-latency window)Must not look frozen — the state most likely to get a second tap from an impatient guest
SpeakingDelivering a responseNeeds visible mouth movement (viseme/lip-sync) — a believable talk-cycle, not just an open/closed toggle

Every state must also hold up with reduced text size on, and with a subtitle bar present — design with the subtitle area reserved, not added after.

2. Visual direction — human, stylized rather than photoreal

Confirmed: human-like, not abstract. Specifically a stylized/illustrated human character (flat-vector app-mascot register, not a photograph), weighed against two heavier alternatives:

  • Photoreal animated portrait (a real/AI-generated photo, lip-synced via a model like MuseTalk) — looks the most like an actual person, but requires sourcing a specific face (licensing/likeness implications) and more GPU headroom.
  • Full photoreal 3D (e.g. NVIDIA ACE/Audio2Face driving an Unreal Engine MetaHuman) — the most realistic option, but needs a dedicated rendering GPU separate from the one running the LLM, plus a full 3D content pipeline. Most reference builds in this category are designed cloud-first, which cuts against "no guest data leaves the property."

A stylized illustrated character sidesteps both: it's a 2D rigged character, animates cheaply, never competes for GPU with the LLM/STT/TTS stack, and avoids the steepest uncanny-valley risk. Persona direction (open for the first concept pass): warm, professional, approachable — reads as "hotel concierge." Keep facial features simple/universal rather than tied to one specific ethnicity or real person, since guests across many nationalities will be looking at the same face.

3. Technical direction: Live2D-style rigged 2D character

Recommendation: build as a Live2D-style rigged character — the standard tool for a flat illustrated character with layered parts that deform and animate in real time, including lip-sync via mouth-shape swapping driven by audio.

  • Build as layered 2D artwork (separate layers per deformable part: eyes, mouth, eyebrows) rigged with a mesh/bone system, not a flat static image or a pre-rendered video loop.
  • Lip-sync is now in scope — as viseme-driven mouth-shape swapping (a handful of discrete mouth shapes mapped to phoneme groups), not phoneme-perfect motion capture.
  • Runs on CPU/light GPU — no AI video-generation model needed at runtime.
  • Deliver source files (Live2D rig or equivalent) sized for a tablet-resolution canvas, not print/marketing resolution.

4. Deliverables expected from this brief

  • Two or three quick concept directions for the character itself (styling, gender presentation, exact look) — low-fidelity, for a go/no-go decision before investing in a full rig.
  • Once direction is picked: the five states above as a rigged, lip-synced Live2D character.
  • A static "hero frame" per state, for dropping into the hi-fi mockups.
